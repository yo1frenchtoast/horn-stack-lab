Horn and bass-reflex PA stack simulator, computed from Hornresp scripts: Cubo 18, Cubo Sub, Kick, MT130, MHB, E15, GK MB112 and more. Stack the cabinets in a column or a pyramid, aim them, set filters, delays, gains and equaliser, add your own drivers, then read the response at the listening spot (SPL, phase, group delay, excursion, impedance), the waves in 2D or 3D and a simulated listening.

General settings iThe number of groups repeats the whole configuration side by side. Each cabinet receives the same signal as the others of its way (one amp channel per cabinet). Click on the map to move the listening spot.

Stack editor iThe top row of the table is the top of the stack. Numbers decreasing upwards give a symmetric pyramid, each row being centred on x = 0. For 2 subs then 2 subs, add two Cubo 18 stages. Each stage's filters are set in the cards below. Speakers marked "estimated" (MHB, Kick Planar BR, HBR212, HD15, SAKB28, Invader Bin, BPH118, HR118, S218 NTLW, Cubo 12, Audiophony E15 and its HF way, Audiophony CB118) are rebuilt from the plans: equivalent horn, proxy driver and approximate volumes. The curves are indicative; replace them with a real Hornresp file if you have one.

Speaker driver library iThe horn, chambers and port stay those of the enclosure; only the driver changes. To keep good coupling, the driver's Sd must stay close to the horn throat (or its front chamber). Generics are typical values, not measurements.

 pick a driver per stage in its enclosure, or add your own from its Thiele-Small parameters
Or paste a Hornresp block (Sd, Bl, Cms, Rms, Mmd, Le, Re, Pmax, Xmax)

Equaliser (before the crossover filters) iThe equaliser acts on the common signal, before the split into three ways: it therefore changes all three ways at once, then come the crossover filters, the gain and the delay of each way. It counts in the curves, the map and the simulated listening. Drag the points on the curve or set the bands in the table. Classic biquad filters (RBJ formulas) computed at 48 kHz. Auto-EQ measures the system sum at the listening spot (without the equaliser), smooths it to third-octave, then places up to 7 bells to bring it closer to the target over the chosen range. Redo it after moving the listening spot or changing the stack.

Wave map iThe map sums the mouths as pistons in a baffle (directivity of a piston of the same area around each cabinet's axis, nothing to the rear, no floor reflection). Azimuth, tilt and splay rotate this axis. The horn calculation follows Hornresp's plane-wave model. It does not replace a boundary-element simulation.

Hover the map to read the coordinates (x: lateral, y: distance in front of the face, z: height) and the level.

Simulated listening iThe signal is filtered by the system's complex response (level and phase) at the listening spot, with the filters, delays and positions set above. The level is calibrated at first start and then kept: adding subs or changing a gain is audible. A limiter protects your ears. The spectrum is measured at the output of the simulated system, before the volume and the limiter (dB relative to digital full scale). No room, no distortion, no power compression. Your headphones or computer speakers do not go down into the bass: what is below 60 to 80 Hz will not be heard, even if the calculation is right.

Press Play. Start with a low volume: the bass can climb fast.
Hover the spectrum to read the frequency and the level. Solid: system output, dotted: source, thin line: peaks. Drag across the spectrum to isolate a frequency range (steep band-pass on the simulated system).
